# Tennessee Permit Awareness Procedure

> **Working operational guidance, not legal advice.** Confirm every job with the governing county, city, utility, and agency before relying on a permit determination. The customer or developer may own an approval, but Noland Earthworks should not begin work until the permittee, scope, and conditions are documented.

## The practical answer

There is no single statewide “forestry mulching permit” identified in Tennessee’s published permit programs. The requirement turns on **what the work disturbs, where it occurs, and whether it is tied to development**. A surface-only mulching job on upland private land is materially different from clearing that exposes soil, is part of a subdivision, crosses a stream, changes an entrance, or takes place in a floodplain.

The best protection is a **permit-release gate before mobilization**, not a last-minute permit search after the quote is accepted. Treat a job as “screening required” when it involves more than ordinary surface mulching, is near water, involves 1 acre or more of construction-related disturbance, occurs in a municipality, creates or changes access, or is part of a larger development plan.

## Pre-quote intake: six questions that trigger the right checks

| Ask before quoting | Why it matters | Next step if yes or unknown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is the exact property address, county, city, and Parcel ID? | Local rules, floodplain administration, and land-disturbance thresholds vary by jurisdiction. | Identify the county and incorporated city; call the local planning/stormwater office. |
| Is this surface mulching only, or does scope include grubbing, stumps below grade, grading, fill, drainage, road work, trenching, or ponds? | These activities can change the permitting and TN811 analysis. | Treat as a red flag; confirm local land-disturbance and TDEC requirements before scheduling. |
| How many acres will be disturbed now, and is it part of a subdivision, commercial project, or other larger plan? | Tennessee construction stormwater coverage may apply at 1 acre or more, including smaller work within a larger common plan. | Obtain the project’s TDEC/local permit status and identify the actual operator/permittee. |
| Are there streams, creeks, ditches with ordinary flow, wetlands, springs, ponds, lake shorelines, sinkholes, or mapped floodplain near the work area? | Water-resource and floodplain approvals can apply even when acreage is small. | Stop and obtain a written screening/determination from the appropriate local office or TDEC. |
| Are utilities, septic, wells, irrigation, buried lines, overhead lines, or utility easements present? | Utility safety is a mandatory pre-work issue whenever the scope is excavation or demolition. | Obtain a TN811 ticket and written owner information for private utilities/easements. |
| Will the work create, change, or use a driveway/entrance on a state route, public right-of-way, or utility corridor? | Access work and work outside private property commonly need separate approval. | Obtain written authorization; for state highways, the property owner must address TDOT entrance permitting. |

## Permit and approval triggers to treat as red flags

| Trigger | What to verify | Authoritative starting point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 acre or more of clearing, grading, or excavation**, or less than 1 acre within a larger development plan | Tennessee Construction General Permit coverage, Notice of Intent, SWPPP, local permit, and who is the legal operator/permittee. | TDEC says construction sites involving clearing, grading, or excavation with 1 acre or more of disturbance—and smaller portions of a larger common plan—need construction stormwater coverage. [1] |
| **City or county land disturbance** | Local grading/erosion/land-disturbance permit threshold, exemptions, inspection requirements, and required erosion control. | Check the property’s local planning/stormwater office. For example, Davidson County regulates removal of ground cover and has thresholds/exemptions different from the statewide 1-acre trigger; Rutherford County requires a local land-disturbance permit for construction activity over 1 acre. [2] [3] |
| **Stream, wetland, lake, pond, drainage crossing, fill, bank work, or water diversion** | TDEC ARAP or §401 process and any U.S. Army Corps §404 requirement. Do not assume vegetation-only work is exempt when equipment will alter a water resource. | TDEC requires ARAP/§401 approval for physical alterations to streams, rivers, lakes, and wetlands; examples include filling/draining wetlands, road crossings, and bank work. [4] |
| **Floodplain, drainageway, or sinkhole/wet-weather area** | Local floodplain development permit and local stormwater rules. | Floodplain requirements are administered locally; TEMA directs owners to the local floodplain administrator before building, filling, grading, or improvements near mapped floodplains. [5] |
| **Excavation, grading, scraping, stumping below grade, trenching, or demolition** | TN811 ticket, mark validity, private-line responsibility, and field-safe work area. | Tennessee requires notice 3–10 working days before excavation/demolition and each responsible excavator/demolisher must obtain a locate ticket. [6] |
| **New or altered entrance on a state highway** | TDOT highway entrance permit, scope, and owner responsibility. | TDOT requires a permit before constructing, modifying, revising, or changing use of a driveway on state highway right-of-way. [7] |
| **Large contract or development-related scope** | Whether the project falls within Tennessee contractor licensing rules, including the classification and monetary limit. This is not a site permit, but it is a pre-contract compliance gate. | Tennessee requires a contractor license before bidding, offering, or negotiating projects of $25,000 or more; do not divide a project into phases to avoid the rule. Confirm the application of this rule to the exact scope with the Board. [8] |

## The 15-minute permit screen before a site visit

1. **Open the parcel and map the work footprint.** Record Parcel ID, address, county, city, acreage, intended work area, and the customer’s stated purpose. Keep photos and a marked aerial image in the quote file.
2. **Classify the scope honestly.** Mark `surface mulching only` only when no soil-moving, grubbing, road, drainage, fill, grading, stump removal below grade, or stream/wetland work is included. If the customer adds any of those later, reopen screening.
3. **Run the water screen.** Review aerials, the National Wetlands Inventory, local GIS, flood maps, and obvious field indicators. These are screening tools—not final determinations. TDEC states that an on-site inspection is necessary to determine wetlands with certainty. [9]
4. **Run the jurisdiction screen.** Determine whether the site is in an incorporated city, a special flood hazard area, a subdivision/common development plan, a conservation easement, an HOA, a utility easement, or beside a state route.
5. **Call before committing.** For any red flag, send a short written question to the local planning/stormwater or floodplain office and, for water-resource issues, the appropriate TDEC field office. Include address/Parcel ID, map, acres, equipment, and exact scope. Save the response in the job folder.
6. **Release only with documentation.** Record the agency contact, date, case/ticket number, answer, permit number if any, permittee/operator, conditions, and expiration date. “Customer said no permit is needed” is not enough.

## Who should obtain the permit?

Default to this position in your proposal: **the owner/developer is responsible for securing and paying for permits, approvals, utility/easement permissions, and project plans before mobilization; Noland Earthworks supplies the exact work scope and cooperates with reasonable documentation.** Do not rely on that clause alone. For a TDEC construction permit, the legal “operator” can depend on actual control of the site and stormwater compliance, and the Notice of Intent is not transferable. Confirm the named permittee/operator directly with the agency before work begins. [1]

For a red-flag project, price the work as **“subject to receipt and review of required approvals.”** If the customer wants Noland Earthworks to handle applications, make that a separately priced administrative scope after confirming who legally may apply and what plans, engineering, inspections, or certifications are required.

## Proposal and work-order language to adopt

> **Permits, approvals, and site conditions.** Owner is responsible for obtaining and paying for all required permits, land-disturbance approvals, stormwater plans, utility/easement permissions, access approvals, and other governmental or private authorizations before work begins. Owner shall provide copies before mobilization. Noland Earthworks will perform only the stated forestry mulching and land-management scope and will not perform grading, excavation, hauling, drainage, utility, road, or water-resource alteration work unless separately agreed in writing. Work may be delayed or suspended if required approvals, safe utility clearances, or site conditions are not documented.

Have Tennessee counsel review this once, then use the approved version consistently.

## Simple operating rule

**Green light:** private upland work, clearly surface mulching only, no water/floodplain/easement/utility/access red flags, and the local jurisdiction confirms no permit concern.

**Yellow light:** unclear parcel limits, acreage close to thresholds, municipal location, nearby water, field evidence of drainage/saturation, utility uncertainty, or property part of a development plan. Do not schedule until the calls are documented.

**Red light:** 1+ acres of construction-related disturbance, work in or across water/wetlands, floodplain work, grading/fill/drainage, new state-road access, or missing 811 clearance for excavation-type scope. Require written agency direction and permits before accepting a start date.
