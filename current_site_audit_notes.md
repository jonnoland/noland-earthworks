# Current Site Audit Notes — August 2026

## Observed public conversion strengths

- The homepage has immediate owner-operated positioning, a visible phone number, a quote CTA, pricing-tool entry point, service navigation, service-area verification, and a mobile chat entry point.
- The quote form sets a clear four-step expectation: review request, schedule a site visit, receive written estimate, no obligation.
- The form collects service, county, client type, acreage, address, and project details, which supports qualification without requiring a customer to understand technical field terms.
- The quote page now states that active-duty/veteran and first-time customer discounts may apply after a site assessment and that only one qualifying discount applies.

## Observed issues to evaluate further

- The public navigation exposes an **Ops** button on the quote page while an authenticated owner session is active. Verify that it remains hidden for public visitors and does not create a distracting public-path element.
- The homepage still uses **Testimonials** as a navigation label while verified Google review data is currently unavailable. Keep the section free of unsupported review-style claims and consider renaming it to real-project proof if it is not live-review content.
- The form offers acreage bands below the stated one-acre starting point. The new explanation helps, but low-fit form submissions should be measured and possibly routed into a lighter qualification path.
- Service naming needs consolidation: the quote form includes Property Maintenance and Trail Cutting, while the primary public service structure emphasizes Forestry Mulching, Land Management, Vegetation Management, Right-of-Way Clearing, and Site Preparation.
- The footer/header present both `quotes@nolandearthworks.com` and `info@nolandearthworks.com`; retain the distinction, but make the role of each address explicit to reduce customer uncertainty.

## Observed operations dashboard findings

- The dashboard has a strong navigation structure, live operational KPIs, a visible Today’s Next Actions queue, review-request tracking, lead-generation milestones, and direct links from action items to the correct sales records.
- The action queue identifies a **315-hour-old lead** and three unsent quotes. This is the clearest current sales-execution constraint; more marketing will not compensate for stale response and quote-send gaps.
- The dashboard shows four open quotes, but their compact cards display blank quote number/date/total fields. This weakens scanability and suggests that optional quote data is not consistently populated or formatted.
- Paid This Month displays **$0.5** rather than a whole-dollar business amount. This is a data-unit or formatter defect that undermines confidence in the KPI layer and should be treated as a reporting-data quality issue.
- Requests are labelled **Unknown Service** for several entries. Lead capture is working, but required qualification fields or source mapping are not consistently producing a usable service classification.
- The daily milestone snapshot is active and saved. The dashboard now provides usable visibility, but targets should remain decision-oriented: response within 24 hours, quote sent, quote viewed, approved, and review request sent.

## Observed trust and proof findings

- The reviews page is honest: it explicitly says it publishes verified feedback only and does not use placeholder reviews. Retain this policy.
- The reviews page still uses the phrase **“verified review feed is being connected”** even though the integration is working and Google currently has no public rating or reviews. Replace that copy with a direct, permanent state such as “Verified Google reviews will appear here as they are received.”
- The homepage and footer still use the **Testimonials** label, while the public reviews page has no verified testimonials. Rename those navigation references to **Why Landowners Choose Us** or **Our Work** until genuine reviews are available.
- The gallery is a strong asset: it uses real before/after pairs, service labels, county references, acreage on several jobs, and job-specific descriptions.
- Gallery taxonomy is inconsistent with the current service strategy: the cards and filters use **Land Clearing** and **Brush Hogging**, while primary public positioning uses Land Management and forestry mulching. Consolidate labels so the customer sees one service vocabulary.
- Gallery content is still static and limited. The highest-value addition is a repeatable job-story format: location area, scope, acreage/linear feet, vegetation/access challenge, work completed, time on site, and a CTA to request a similar site visit.
