# Permit, Approval, Access, and Owner Responsibilities

> **Draft — have Tennessee counsel review before using as a standard contract term.**

**Permits, Approvals, and Site Authorization.** Owner represents that Owner has the legal authority to authorize the work described in this Agreement and to provide Noland Earthworks, LLC (“Contractor”) access to the property and work area. Before Contractor mobilizes, Owner shall obtain, maintain, and pay for all permits, plans, inspections, approvals, consents, and authorizations required for the stated work, including any applicable land-disturbance, grading, stormwater, erosion and sediment control, environmental, wetland, stream, floodplain, zoning, tree-removal, driveway or right-of-way, utility-easement, homeowners’ association, and local-government requirements. Owner shall provide Contractor copies of all applicable approvals, permit numbers, site plans, permit conditions, and access authorizations before the scheduled start date.

Contractor will perform only the forestry mulching and land-management scope expressly stated in this Agreement. Unless separately agreed in writing, Contractor does not perform or assume responsibility for grading, excavation, hauling, drainage, road construction, utility work, stream or wetland alteration, or other work requiring a separate scope, permit, design, or specialized approval. Contractor’s discussion of possible permit requirements is not legal, engineering, environmental, or permitting advice and does not relieve Owner of Owner’s responsibilities under this section.

Owner shall identify property boundaries, easements, private utilities, septic systems, wells, irrigation, underground improvements, and other site conditions that could affect safe performance of the work. If the stated scope changes, required approvals are missing or expired, site conditions differ materially from information supplied, or Contractor reasonably determines that work cannot proceed safely or in compliance with applicable requirements, Contractor may delay, suspend, or reschedule the work until the issue is resolved. Any resulting additional mobilization, standby, remobilization, or change-scope cost must be approved in writing before Contractor proceeds.
