# Noland Earthworks Comprehensive Website & Operations Audit

**Audit date:** August 12, 2026  
**Scope:** Public website, quote flow, reviews and gallery, SEO/AEO, live operations dashboard, lead and quote data, AI workflows, and current local-service research.

## Executive Assessment

The website has a stronger foundation than the current quote volume suggests. It is clear about the owner-operated model, has a mobile-first quote route, credible job photography, strong county coverage, a detailed operations system, and useful AI tools that already support qualification, estimates, proposals, photos, pricing, and marketing. The present constraint is **sales execution and trust consistency**, not a shortage of features. Current dashboard evidence shows a 315-hour-old lead, three quote drafts that still need to be reviewed and sent, four open quotes, and no recent review requests. Adding more top-of-funnel AI before those loops are closed would create more work rather than more approved jobs.

The immediate priority is to make every visible claim consistent with verified data, then make the existing lead-to-quote-to-review workflow impossible to overlook. The next priority is to turn real completed jobs into recurring proof: project stories, job-specific media, review requests, local updates, and source-attributed case-study inputs.

## What Is Working Well

The public site communicates a clear operating model. The homepage identifies Jon as the owner and operator, states that estimates are on-site rather than phone-based, explains the mulch-on-site approach, and provides prominent quote and phone actions. The quote form collects service, county, customer type, approximate acreage, address, and scope details. That is enough qualification information to decide whether a site visit makes sense without forcing the prospect to understand equipment or pricing variables.

The gallery is the site’s most credible marketing asset. It uses real before-and-after job pairs with county, acreage in several examples, vegetation conditions, and a practical outcome. This directly supports the local evidence Google recommends businesses maintain through complete information, reviews, and photos or videos.[1] The field data and AI photo-caption workflow give the business a realistic way to add new material without making social posting a separate evening task.

The operations system also has real value. The dashboard now combines pipeline, quote, invoice, review, and milestone information with direct action links. Lead scoring, AI estimate generation, proposal drafting, quote creation, gallery captions, pricing support, review-request safeguards, and AI visibility auditing are all connected to business outcomes rather than being isolated demos. The new daily snapshots are a practical foundation for distinguishing seasonal demand from response, conversion, or tracking failures.

## Critical Risks and Trust Issues

### 1. Remove or verify the homepage’s **4.9 Google Rating** immediately

The homepage currently presents **“4.9★ Google Rating”** while the live reviews page reports that no verified Google rating or reviews are available. This is an observed contradiction on the live site. Even if the rating came from an earlier listing state, it should not be shown unless the current Business Profile supports it. Replace this stat with a factual company attribute such as **Veteran-Owned & Operated**, **35 Counties Served**, or **Free On-Site Estimates** until the verified Google feed returns a rating.

> Google identifies reviews, positive ratings, photos, and complete Business Profile information as part of the local prominence and relevance picture; claims about those signals must match what customers can verify.[1]

The reviews page itself now does the right thing by declining to publish placeholder feedback. Keep that policy. Do not fill the gap with invented testimonials, static review cards, or unverified star ratings.

### 2. Rename **Testimonials** until genuine testimonials exist

The primary navigation, footer, and homepage still use the word **Testimonials**, but the public reviews page correctly has no verified customer feedback to display. Rename this item to **Our Work**, **Why Landowners Choose Us**, or link it to the gallery. This is not a cosmetic issue; it removes an expectation the site cannot yet meet and directs visitors toward the strongest available proof.

### 3. Correct owner-versus-crew language

The homepage hero identifies Jon as the person performing every job, while the process section says, “Our crew arrives on time.” For a solo owner-operator, use one truthful voice: **“I arrive with the right equipment”** or **“I show up with the right equipment.”** The owner-operated position is a differentiator; generic crew language weakens it.

### 4. Repair reporting data before using it to make marketing decisions

The live dashboard displayed **Paid This Month: $0.5**, blank quote metadata on several cards, and multiple **Unknown Service** requests. Those are data-quality or formatter defects. They make a lean one-person operation less able to trust its own dashboard. The next change should standardize cents-to-dollars conversion, require or infer service type during lead creation, and ensure quote cards show a populated number, sent date, and total.

## Lead Generation Findings

### The site needs more proof distribution, not more generic pages

The website already contains a quote form, service pages, county pages, a pricing guide, FAQ content, a gallery, blog resources, and an AI chat route. The next acquisition gain will come from shipping **fresh, location-specific job evidence** from real work: an after photo, short owner explanation, job type, county or nearby community, acreage or linear feet when appropriate, scope boundary, and a direct site-visit CTA.

Current local-search guidance emphasizes complete business information, reviews, and photos or video. Google says local results are mainly driven by relevance, distance, and prominence, with prominence informed by factors such as links and reviews.[1] The practical implication is not “publish more county pages.” It is “attach genuine local evidence to the pages, profile, and media the business already has.”

| Lead-generation issue | Observed evidence | Improvement | Measurement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weak third-party proof | No public rating or reviews currently returned | Build a completed-job review workflow around final payment and manually monitor delivery | Verified review count, review-request send rate, profile calls/clicks |
| Static proof library | Gallery has strong but limited static examples | Add one real project story after each suitable job | New proof items per month, gallery-to-quote clicks |
| Inconsistent service language | Land Management, Land Clearing, Brush Hogging, Property Maintenance, and Trail Cutting appear in different places | Publish a controlled service taxonomy and map old labels to it | Form completion by service, service-page conversion |
| Weak attribution | Recent lead estimated values are zero and sources are sparse | Require source, service, estimated range, and outcome fields before a lead is closed | Source-to-quote and source-to-won value |
| Limited media discovery | YouTube and social links exist but job-story workflow is not visible as a system | Publish short, factual machine-in-action and before/after clips from real jobs | Video views, branded searches, assisted quote requests |

The review research supports a practical reputation plan rather than a mass-review tactic. BrightLocal’s 2025 survey found consumers value detailed experiences and supporting photos or video, while a meaningful share will consider a local business with fewer reviews.[3] That favors a steady post-job request process and detailed, real proof over chasing a large review count quickly.

## Customer Interaction and Sales Execution

The strongest conversion opportunity is inside the existing pipeline. The dashboard currently shows a lead outside the 24-hour response target and three quotes waiting to be reviewed and sent. These are controllable decisions. Before increasing ad spend or building another intake channel, establish an owner habit: first review the **Today’s Next Actions** queue, then send, schedule, follow up, or close each item with a reason.

| Workflow stage | What works now | What needs improvement | Recommended action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lead intake | Detailed quote form, AI scoring, address and acreage context | Some entries still lack a service classification and estimated value | Require service selection or map “multiple/unknown” into a review category; save AI estimate midpoint to the lead |
| First response | 24-hour promise and AI drafting tools exist | Current stale lead shows the promise is not consistently executed | Add a same-day response timer, reason code for deferral, and one-tap SMS/email draft from the queue |
| Quote preparation | AI estimate, proposal draft, native quote creation, portal support | Three drafts are not yet sent; draft creation is being mistaken for sales progress | Treat **Sent** as the sales milestone, not Draft; add a quote-send checklist and confirmation state |
| Quote follow-up | Queue now surfaces quotes; legacy follow-up table shows no active records | Follow-up is not reliably recorded against native quotes | Create native quote follow-up tasks at 2 and 7 business days, with stop conditions for viewed, approved, declined, or closed-lost |
| Final payment and review | Completed-job invoice flow and review agent exist | No review requests sent on the dashboard | When final payment is confirmed, require either “request sent,” “suppressed with reason,” or “manual follow-up scheduled” |

The quote page is generally strong, but the acreage choices below one acre need a deliberately different journey. The message that most work begins at one acre is useful, but visitors can still choose very small ranges. Keep the options for legitimate fence-line or add-on work, but route sub-acre selections into an explicit **mobilization-fit review** rather than treating them as normal acreage jobs. This reduces low-fit workload without appearing dismissive.

The discount copy is appropriately restrained: it says a qualifying discount may be applied after site assessment and does not promise a final price. Keep the one-discount-only rule. Put the details in the internal quote flow, not in a public rate table.

## AI and AEO Findings

The AI system is broad enough. It includes lead qualification, AI quote estimates, proposal drafts, voice-to-bid, photo captions, social drafts, pricing support, ad drafting, AI visibility monitoring, customer chat, review safeguards, and operational summaries. The next improvement is **not another assistant**. It is ensuring every AI output enters an accountable work queue, has an owner approval point, and produces an observable funnel event.

| AI outcome area | Current strength | Improvement needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acquisition | Chat, calculator, content and ad tools | Track whether chat/calculator sessions become qualified leads, not only whether AI responds |
| Sales | Estimate, proposal, and quote creation are connected | Measure estimate saved → quote sent → portal viewed → approved; surface stalled transitions daily |
| Operations | Photo captions, invoicing, review safeguards, field tools | Standardize a completed-job content checklist so real job facts become a gallery entry, social draft, and review trigger when appropriate |
| Intelligence | AI Visibility Score, pricing research, lead visibility dashboard | Treat internal AI scores as diagnostic signals only; use external citations, Search Console, Business Profile actions, and won work as validation |

For AEO, continue using concise answers, headings, tables, real field detail, and consistent service entities. Bing’s AI Performance tools now expose cited pages and grounding queries, making cited URLs and query patterns more useful than a self-contained “visibility score.”[4] Set Bing Webmaster Tools and Bing Places as a measurement action, then use cited page data to improve the exact content that is being referenced or missed.

Do **not** prioritize FAQ schema as a traffic feature. Google’s 2026 documentation updates say FAQ rich-result support was deprecated and removed from Google Search.[2] Keep FAQs because they help landowners understand exclusions, site visits, mulching versus bush hogging, terrain, and scheduling. Remove any internal score or recommendation that suggests FAQPage schema itself will earn a Google visual result.
